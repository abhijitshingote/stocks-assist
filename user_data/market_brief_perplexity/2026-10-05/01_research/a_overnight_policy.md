## AI Regulation / **NVDA**, **META**, **GOOGL**, **MSFT**, **OPENAI** [AI Compute · Internet & Platforms · Software & SaaS]

- President Donald Trump announced on October 4 that he appointed National Intelligence Director Jay Clayton, Federal Trade Commission Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor, and Pentagon research and technology official Emil Michael to lead a federal “Super Intelligence Force” coordinating government engagement with AI companies, consumers, public-interest groups, critical-infrastructure providers, and religious organizations; the force will report to Trump and White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles. [report] (Reuters, Oct. 4; updated Oct. 5 00:03 ET)
- Trump said the task force would “ensure that America continues to lead the World in Super Intelligence,” which he described as potentially larger than the Industrial Revolution and the internet. (AP, Oct. 4; updated Oct. 5 05:25 ET)
- Trump’s October 4 announcement followed a September 29 White House meeting with AI executives from Nvidia, SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, and Alphabet’s Google, at which participating companies signed a voluntary accord calling for robust internal controls and independent external audits. (Reuters, Oct. 4; updated Oct. 5 00:03 ET)
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Axios that AI companies should police themselves and criticized warnings about existential AI risks; when asked about calls from AI executives for regulation, Bessent said, “Well, then they should slow down.” (Axios, Oct. 3; updated Oct. 4 20:03 ET)
- Bessent said the United States and China planned a bilateral AI-notification system intended to share warnings about serious incidents and reduce uncertainty; he said the Treasury Department and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng had agreed to create a “U.S.-China AI dialogue.” (Axios, Oct. 3; updated Oct. 4 20:03 ET)

## **XLE**, **VLO**, **MPC**, **PSX** / Europe Diesel Reserves [Energy]

- President Donald Trump said on October 2 that European countries had agreed to release diesel reserves after EU governments discussed a French proposal for Europe to release 50 million barrels of diesel and for International Energy Agency members to release 50 million barrels of crude oil. [report] (Reuters, Oct. 2 11:13 ET)
- Reuters reported that the United States had urged France and Germany to release emergency diesel inventories and had sought releases totaling 100 million barrels within 20 days; the discussions followed US consideration of a diesel-export ban amid fuel-price disruptions linked to the Iran war. [report] (Reuters, Oct. 2 11:13 ET)
- The Group of Seven agreed to release 100 million barrels of crude oil and fuel from emergency reserves, according to a trade publication report dated October 3; the report said the agreement reduced the immediate risk of a European and Mexican supply disruption. [report] (Washington Trade & Tariff, Oct. 3; updated Oct. 5 07:47 ET)
