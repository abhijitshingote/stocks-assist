## Brazil presidential election [Election]

- Flávio Bolsonaro received **47.04% of valid votes** and Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva received **45.15%**, with a **1.89-percentage-point** gap and **2,252,566 votes** between them after **99.96%** of polling stations had reported. (Rio Times, Oct. 5, 09:31 ET)
- The election was a **first-round result**; neither candidate reached the required **50%** threshold, so Bolsonaro and Lula advanced to a **runoff on October 25, 2026**. (Associated Press, Oct. 5, 09:27 ET; Reuters, Oct. 5, 07:10 ET)
- Reuters reported the initial result as approximately **47% for Bolsonaro** and **44.9% for Lula**, with the runoff scheduled for **October 25**. (Reuters, Oct. 5, 07:10 ET)

## ITUB / BBD / EWZ [Equities]

- Overseas-listed **Itaú Unibanco** and **Banco Bradesco** shares rose more than **9% in pre-market trading**, while the **MSCI Brazil ETF** rose more than **14%** after Bolsonaro led the first round. (Investing.com, Oct. 5, 08:35 ET)
- A separate pre-market market note reported **ITUB and BBD up more than 13%** and **EWZ up 12%** by the morning session; the available search results did not provide a named exchange timestamp or a firm-specific analyst rating/price-target change for those moves. 
- **Nu Holdings** rose **10.5%**, **Vale** rose **8%**, and **Ambev** rose **7%** in U.S. trading after the election result. (Investing.com, Oct. 5, 08:35 ET)
- RBC BlueBay senior sovereign strategist **Graham Stock** said local markets could perform well after the first-round result and assigned Bolsonaro a **47:45** first-round lead, while projecting a potential runoff margin based on transferred votes; he did not provide a stock-specific rating or price target. (Reuters, Oct. 5, 10:15 ET)
- Societe Generale forecast the Brazilian real at **5.10 per U.S. dollar by the end of 2026**, with potential to move below **5.00 per dollar in the first half of 2027**; no stock-specific rating or price target was reported. (Reuters, Oct. 5, 10:45 ET)

## Brazilian fiscal and monetary policy [Policy]

- **Graham Stock**, senior sovereign strategist at RBC BlueBay, said a Bolsonaro presidency could increase the probability of **fiscal constraints**, but said Brazil’s Congress was still unlikely to be fiscally conservative; he did not provide a fiscal-adjustment estimate or stock price target. (Reuters, Oct. 5, 10:15 ET)
- **Reuters’ investor-reaction report** said analysts expected a Bolsonaro administration to announce a **fiscal adjustment** quickly; the expected market effect depended on whether the adjustment was credible and supported by Brazil’s institutional configuration. (Reuters, Oct. 5, 10:15 ET)
- **Alberto Ramos**, chief Latin America economist at Goldman Sachs, said the immediate market read-through was higher probability of **fiscal consolidation and structural reform**; he said disciplined execution could lower sovereign risk premia, improve debt dynamics and support capital inflows. (Reuters, Oct. 5, 10:15 ET)
- Ramos said Brazil could potentially **stabilize its credit rating** under a Bolsonaro presidency, but that clarity would come only after the October 25 runoff. (Reuters, Oct. 5, 10:15 ET)
- Reuters reported that the sell-side expectation was for a **4%–5% move in foreign exchange**, a **7%–8% rise in equities**, and a stronger **central-bank rate-cut cycle** under a Bolsonaro victory; the report did not identify the individual sell-side firms behind that forecast in the available results. (Reuters, Oct. 5, 10:15 ET)
- **Luis Chermont**, chief investment officer at Itajubá Investimentos, forecast a **2%–4% fall in the dollar**, a **4%–6% rise in equities**, and tighter nominal and real interest rates after Bolsonaro’s first-round lead. (Reuters, Oct. 5, 10:45 ET)
