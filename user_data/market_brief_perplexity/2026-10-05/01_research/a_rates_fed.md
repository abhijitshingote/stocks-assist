## Rates & Fed `[Macro]`

- The **2-year Treasury yield** was **4.797%**, down **2 bp**, at approximately **08:16 ET on Monday, October 5**. (CNBC, Oct 5 08:16 ET)
- The **10-year Treasury yield** was **5.255%**, down **more than 1 bp**, at approximately **08:16 ET on Monday, October 5**. (CNBC, Oct 5 08:16 ET)
- The **30-year Treasury yield** was **5.614%**, down **1 bp**, at approximately **08:16 ET on Monday, October 5**. (CNBC, Oct 5 08:16 ET)
- On Friday, October 2, the **2-year yield** closed at **4.718%**, down **6.86 bp**; the **10-year yield** closed at **5.178%**, down **5.6 bp**; and the **30-year yield** closed at **5.5704%**, down **3.26 bp**. (Reuters, Oct 2; republished Oct 4 19:00 ET)
- The **2s10s spread** was approximately **+46 bp** on Friday, October 2. (Reuters, Oct 2; republished Oct 4 19:00 ET)
- CME FedWatch indicated an approximately **82% probability that the Fed would leave rates unchanged** at its next meeting as of Monday morning, October 5. (CNBC, Oct 5 08:16 ET)
- Chicago Fed President **Austan Goolsbee** said on Friday, October 2: “There is plenty of room for anything to be on the table,” when asked whether the Fed should raise rates or pause in October. (Reuters, Oct 2)
- Goolsbee also said: “Let’s see if we can get some evidence put together that suggests we’re headed back to 2% inflation.” (Reuters, Oct 2)
- New York Fed President **John Williams** said: “Given the policy decision we made at our September meeting, there is no urgent need to act and we have time to gather more information.” (Reuters, published Oct 5)
- Williams said the Fed could wait for more data while still expecting **one additional rate increase before year-end**. (Reuters, published Oct 5)
- Fed Vice Chair **Philip Jefferson** said: “My colleagues and I will need to come to our own judgment, which may take more time.” (Reuters, published Oct 5)
- Jefferson said future policy adjustments should be determined by “carefully examining trends in the data, the evolving outlook, and the balance…” (Reuters, published Oct 5)
- Dallas Fed President **Lorie Logan** said rates may need to rise by an additional **50 bp or more**. (Saxo Bank, Oct 5 10:18 ET)
- Fed Vice Chair for Supervision **Michelle Bowman** signaled that there was **no urgent need for further rate action**. (Saxo Bank, Oct 5 10:18 ET)
- The next FOMC meeting was scheduled for **October 27–28**, with the policy announcement due at **14:00 ET on Wednesday, October 28**. (OpenBook Analytics, Oct 2 18:54 ET)
