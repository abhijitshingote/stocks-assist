## Accenture [Earnings]

- Accenture reported fiscal Q4 2026 revenue of **$18.68 billion**, up **6% in U.S. dollars** and **7% in local currency**, versus consensus of **$18.03 billion**; adjusted EPS was **$3.29** versus consensus of **$3.18–$3.19**. (Accenture release, Oct. 1; CNBC, Oct. 1)
- Q4 new bookings were **$22.17 billion**, up **4% in U.S. dollars** and **5% in local currency**, with a **1.2 book-to-bill ratio**. (Accenture release, Oct. 1)
- Q4 managed-services bookings were **$12.77–$12.8 billion**, with a **1.4 book-to-bill ratio**; consulting bookings were **$9.40 billion**. (Accenture release, Oct. 1)
- Q4 consulting revenue was **$9.28 billion**, up **6%** in U.S. dollars; managed-services revenue was **$9.40 billion**, up **7%**. (Accenture release, Oct. 1)
- Accenture’s Q4 revenue exceeded its prior guidance range of **$17.75 billion to $18.40 billion**. (Accenture release, Oct. 1)
- For fiscal 2027, Accenture guided to **3%–6% revenue growth in local currency**, **GAAP diluted EPS of $14.39–$14.81**, and at least **$9.5 billion** of cash returned to shareholders. (Accenture release, Oct. 1)
- Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue guidance was **$18.95 billion–$19.60 billion**, equivalent to **2%–6% local-currency growth** and approximately **1% negative foreign-exchange impact**. (Accenture release, Oct. 1)
- Fiscal 2027 guidance included an expected **2%–2.5% contribution from acquisitions**. (MarketBeat, Oct. 1)
- Accenture reported growth across all three geographic markets and all five industry groups; **Communications, Media & Technology** led industry growth at **10%–11%**, while Products revenue was approximately **$5.6 billion**, Financial Services approximately **$3.5 billion**, Health & Public Service approximately **$3.9 billion**, and Resources approximately **$2.5 billion**. (Accenture release, Oct. 1; Investing.com, Oct. 1)
- The available reports identify no Q4 segment that missed consensus; the published data show an overall revenue and EPS beat, but do not provide a complete analyst-consensus table for each Accenture industry group or service line. (CNBC, Oct. 1; Accenture release, Oct. 1)

## Accenture [AI demand and headcount commentary]

- Accenture said it had nearly **110,000 AI and data professionals** at fiscal year-end 2026, exceeding its prior three-year goal of expanding that workforce from **40,000 to 80,000**. (Accenture earnings-call transcript, Oct. 2)
- More than **400 clients** initiated advanced AI projects during fiscal 2026, including nearly **100 clients in Q4**. (MarketBeat, Oct. 1)
- Management said clients were moving from AI experimentation toward implementation work involving **digital cores, data foundations, and enterprise AI stacks**. (MarketBeat, Oct. 1)
- Accenture said AI-related productivity gains were **deflationary**, but were being offset by increased project scope and new categories of work. (Yahoo Finance earnings-call summary, Oct. 1)
- Management said revenue per person increased in fiscal 2026 and attributed part of that increase to AI-driven productivity. (Yahoo Finance earnings-call summary, Oct. 1)
- Accenture said it expected to hire in every market during fiscal 2027, but at a **lower rate than fiscal 2026**, partly because of AI-driven internal productivity gains. (Yahoo Finance earnings-call summary, Oct. 1)
- Management said it expected to continue hiring entry-level workers while the nature of entry-level work changed. (Yahoo Finance earnings-call highlights, Oct. 1)
- The available reporting does not provide a quantified estimate of traditional IT-services headcount displaced by AI, nor does it identify a specific number of jobs eliminated because of AI. (Accenture earnings-call transcript, Oct. 2)
