## Fed Officials [Macro]

- Federal Reserve Vice Chair **Philip Jefferson** said future policy adjustments should depend on “incoming data, the evolving outlook and the balance of risks”; he said policymakers’ judgment “may take more time” and described the economy as facing a “cascade of shocks” from energy prices, AI infrastructure investment and trade-policy changes. The remarks were delivered Thursday and reported Friday, October 2. (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2, 08:23 ET)[2]

- New York Fed President **John Williams** said there was “no need for urgency” in changing monetary policy and that the Fed had “time to gather more information”; he also said “one further upward adjustment” could be appropriate later in 2026. (CNBC TV18, Oct. 2, publication time not provided)[8]

- Chicago Fed President **Austan Goolsbee** said there was “plenty of room for anything to be on the table” when asked whether the Fed should raise rates or pause at its next meeting. (Reuters, Oct. 2, publication time not provided)[12]

- Dallas Fed President **Lorie Logan** said the federal-funds target range needed to rise by “an additional 50 basis points or more” to balance risks and the Fed’s dual-mandate goals. Her remarks were delivered Thursday and reported during the October 2 window. (Bloomberg, Oct. 2, publication time not provided)[4]

- Minneapolis Fed President **Neel Kashkari** said, “I don’t have a strong view” on whether the next rate increase should occur at the end of October; he said his forecast called for one more rate hike in 2026 and another in 2027. His comments were made Thursday and reported during the window. (CNBC TV18, Oct. 2, publication time not provided)[8]

- Richmond Fed President **Tom Barkin**, Boston Fed President **Susan Collins** and Kansas City Fed President **Jeff Schmid** declined to state their expected policy paths for the Fed’s final two meetings of 2026 at an Asheville, North Carolina conference. (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2, publication time not provided)[3]

- Traders priced approximately a **25% probability** of an October rate increase, down from approximately **70%** earlier in the week, according to federal-funds futures pricing cited in reports published October 2. (CNBC TV18, Oct. 2, publication time not provided)[8]

## Federal Reserve [Macro / Calendar]

- The Federal Reserve Board extended the comment period for its proposal to modernize **Regulation O** until **November 4, 2026**; comments had originally been due October 5. (Federal Reserve, Oct. 2, 16:02 ET)[7]
