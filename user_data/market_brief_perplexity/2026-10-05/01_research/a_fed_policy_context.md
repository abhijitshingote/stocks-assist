## Fed Policy [Macro / Calendar]

- The most recent FOMC decision was a **25-basis-point rate increase** at the September 2026 meeting; all **11 voting FOMC members** supported the increase. The policy rate was raised to combat inflation above the Fed’s 2% target.[9][12] (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 1; Newsquawk, Oct. 2)
- The September FOMC’s updated projections showed **one additional 25-basis-point increase in 2026**, followed by unchanged rates through 2027; Chair Warsh did not submit projections.[12] (Newsquawk, Oct. 2)
- New York Fed President **John Williams** said there was **“no need for urgency”** regarding another October rate increase.[1] (Reuters, Oct. 2, 11:29 ET)
- Minneapolis Fed President **Neel Kashkari** said, **“Inflation is still too high,”** after the August PCE release; he said inflation was running at approximately 3%.[9][15] (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 1; Ground News, Oct. 5, 06:15 ET)
- Mohamed El-Erian said the September employment report would **“put the Fed definitely on hold for October.”**[8] (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2, 09:46 ET)
- The September employment report showed **29,000 jobs added**, versus economists’ expectations for **90,000**, and unemployment of **4.2%**; market-implied odds of an October hike fell to approximately **16%–22%**, depending on the reading and time of publication.[6][8] (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2, 09:46 ET; Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2, 13:04 ET)

## Inflation [Macro / Calendar]

- August headline **PCE inflation was 3.4% year over year**, while **core PCE inflation was 3.0% year over year**.[9] (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 1)
- August core PCE increased **0.2% month over month**, down from **0.4%** previously and below the **0.3% consensus**; the year-over-year rate was **3.0%**, below the **3.3% forecast**.[12] (Newsquawk, Oct. 2)
- August headline CPI increased **3.4% year over year**, according to reporting on the latest inflation data; the release was described as below expectations, with July’s figure revised downward.[1][3] (Reuters, Oct. 2, 11:29 ET; CNBC TV18, Oct. 2)
- The September CPI report was scheduled for **October 14, 2026** and was identified as the next key inflation release for the October policy decision.[12] (Newsquawk, Oct. 2)
- No September CPI, core CPI, or September PCE reading was available within the specified window ending **October 5, 2026 at 12:20 ET**.[12] (Newsquawk, Oct. 2)

## Treasury Yields and Inflation Commentary [Macro / Commodities]

- The 10-year Treasury yield reached **5.34%**, its highest level since **2002**, before declining after the weak September employment report; it later settled near **5.18%**.[1][11] (Reuters, Oct. 2, 11:29 ET; Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2, 19:00 ET)
- The 30-year Treasury yield declined to approximately **5.57%**, while the 2-year yield fell to approximately **4.73%** after the employment report.[11] (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2, 19:00 ET)
- HSBC chief Asia economist **Fred Neumann** said, **“until monetary tightening is delivered, bond markets will demand a premium for longer-term borrowing.”**[5] (CNBC TV18, Oct. 2)
- Pepperstone head of research **Chris Weston** said, **“With the Fed now myopically focused on inflation and price pressures, a hot wages print could prove particularly influential for US rates, Treasuries and the USD.”**[3] (CNBC TV18, Oct. 2)
- El-Erian said the weak employment report would produce a **“retracement in yields for now,”** while describing a **“fundamental issue”** involving the imbalance between borrowers and providers of capital.[14] (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2)
- El-Erian also wrote that the weak employment report would **“reinforce the impact of recent Fedspeak in calming expectations about an October rate hike.”**[13] (Morningstar, Oct. 2)

## Investors and Market Commentators [Macro / Commodities]

- Allianz chief economic adviser **Mohamed El-Erian** said the weak September jobs report would make an October hike unlikely and said the report would put the Fed **“definitely on hold for October.”**[6][8] (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2)
- El-Erian described the IMF statement that bond markets were **“continuing to function in an orderly manner”** as **“highly unusual—and risks raising more questions than it answers.”**[7] (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2)
- The market-implied probability of an October Fed hold rose to approximately **77%–84%** after the employment report, while the implied probability of a hike fell to approximately **16%–22%**.[4][6][11] (CNBC, Oct. 2; Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2)

## Employment and Policy Calendar [Macro / Calendar]

- The September employment report recorded **29,000 payroll gains**, **4.2% unemployment**, and an economist consensus of **90,000 payroll gains**.[8] (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2)
- The FOMC minutes from the September meeting were scheduled for release on **Wednesday, October 7, 2026**.[12] (Newsquawk, Oct. 2)
