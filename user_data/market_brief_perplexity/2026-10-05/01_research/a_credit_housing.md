## Credit markets [Macro]
- Global corporate-bond spreads widened by approximately **5 basis points** through Thursday, October 1, putting risk premiums at their highest level in six months; the report attributed the move to higher bond yields, inflation concerns and record borrowing (Bloomberg, Oct. 2 03:37 ET).[7]
- **High-yield credit** was reported at approximately **7.6%**, while **CCC-rated credit** was approximately **15%** and **private credit** approximately **8.3%**; the figures were attributed to Apollo chief economist **Torsten Slok** (Gokhshtein, Oct. 2; publication time unavailable).[1]
- **CCC spreads** were reported at **1,007 basis points** over Treasuries, up from **860 basis points** at the start of September; the source said the level was the highest since the March 2023 banking crisis (Unbiased Headlines, Oct. 1; outside the requested window, cited only as direct background).[13]
- Apollo chief economist **Torsten Slok** said Treasury yields near **5%** and CCC credit near **15%** had ended the period in which investors accepted low yields for income; the quotation was published in an article dated October 2 (Gokhshtein, Oct. 2; publication time unavailable).[1]

## **FICO**, **TRU**, **EFX** [Credit reporting]
- **Fair Isaac (FICO)** fell **6.6%**, **TransUnion (TRU)** fell **2.4%**, and **Equifax (EFX)** fell **3.4%** in premarket trading after a Bloomberg report said the Federal Housing Finance Agency was considering requiring mortgage lenders selling loans to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to use data from **two credit bureaus instead of three** [report]; FHFA Director **Bill Pulte** could announce the change as soon as October 12 (Reuters market item republished by Bitget, Oct. 2; publication time unavailable).[4]
- The same report said FICO, TransUnion and Equifax had declined **60.9%**, **27.5%** and **35.6%**, respectively, year to date through the prior trading-day close (Bloomberg report cited by Bitget, Oct. 2; publication time unavailable).[4]

## Mortgage rates [Housing]
- The average **30-year fixed mortgage rate** reached **7.28%** on October 1, a three-year high, after rising **62 basis points in five weeks** (Realtor.com, Oct. 5 11:04 ET).[2]
- On October 5, the average conforming **30-year fixed rate** was **7.456%**, up from **7.361%** one week earlier; the average **15-year fixed rate** was **6.613%**, up from **6.599%** (Fortune, Oct. 5 07:10 ET).[3]
- The October 5 average **30-year jumbo mortgage rate** was **7.644%**, compared with **7.415%** one week earlier; the average FHA rate was **6.821%**, VA rate **6.912%**, and USDA rate **6.962%** (Fortune, Oct. 5 07:10 ET).[3]
- The October 5 mortgage-rate figures were sourced to **Mortgage Research Center**; Freddie Mac’s next weekly Primary Mortgage Market Survey release was scheduled for Thursday, October 8 (Fortune, Oct. 5 07:10 ET; Realtor.com, Oct. 5 11:04 ET).[2][3]

## Treasury rates and rate-sensitive housing [Macro / Housing]
- The 10-year Treasury yield rose to **5.262%** on October 2 after trading as low as **5.157%** following the September employment report; the yield was up **2.82 basis points** at the cited reading (Economic Times, Oct. 2 12:21 ET).[11]
- The September employment report showed **29,000** jobs added, versus an expected **84,000**, while unemployment was **4.2%** versus an expected **4.1%**; futures pricing implied a **23%** probability of an October Federal Reserve rate increase, down from **28%** before the report (Yahoo Finance, Oct. 2; publication time unavailable).[15]
- A market report said utilities, real estate and homebuilders weakened as the 10-year Treasury yield reached **5.34%** and the 30-year yield reached **5.69%**; those yield readings were reported as the highest since 2002 for the 30-year bond and the highest in 24 years for the 10-year bond (Philstar, Oct. 5 08:23 ET).[8]
