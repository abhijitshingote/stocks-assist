- **Mary Daly, San Francisco Fed president:** “We are more likely than not to need additional adjustments, but the timing of those adjustments to me is something we’re still deliberating”; she also said, “I don’t feel urgent that we have to decide with a limited set of information” (Reuters interview published Oct 15; republished by bdnews24 at 10:44 ET).[6]
- **Mary Daly:** “Definitely this data point suggests that the risk to inflation remains elevated,” but “risks are not realities,” and “reacting on risk when you have time to make a good decision, to me, feels imprudent” (Reuters interview published Oct 15; republished by bdnews24 at 10:44 ET).[6]
- **Mary Daly:** “Why force urgency when you don’t need to have urgency? Why not take the time to be thoughtful and deliberate? And we have more meetings ahead of us” (Reuters interview published Oct 15; republished by bdnews24 at 10:44 ET).[6]
- **Beth Hammack, Cleveland Fed president:** “What I worry about is, if you get this inflationary mindset, if you get these expectations that these price pressures become more and more persistent, it makes it much harder for monetary policy to do the work that we need to do, and we might need to move even more than if you don't have an inflationary mindset set in” (Reuters, Oct 15, time not stated).[1]
- **Beth Hammack:** “I’m not feeling that there’s restraint coming from financial conditions at this…point in time” (Reuters, Oct 15, time not stated).[1]
- **John Williams, New York Fed president:** “I haven’t changed my view that it may be appropriate to have one more rate hike” this year; he added, “I don’t think at this time I have any reason to believe that we would need to do more” after that (Bloomberg interview reported by Reuters and republished by The Star, Oct 15, time not stated).[3]
- **John Williams:** He estimated the real neutral rate at 1% “or a little above that” (Bloomberg interview reported by Reuters and republished by The Star, Oct 15, time not stated).[3]
