## Oil prices [Energy]

- November 2026 WTI settled at **$97.97 per barrel**, up **$2.88, or 3.03%**, on Thursday, October 15; December 2026 Brent settled at **$105.92**, down **$0.14, or 0.13%**. (Reuters, Oct 15; CNBC, Oct 15 12:09 ET)[2][9]
- At **20:12 ET on October 15**, WTI was reported at **$97.61**, down **$0.36, or 0.37%**, from settlement. (CNBC, Oct 15 12:09 ET, updated approximately 15:09 ET)[9]
- At **20:12 ET on October 15**, December Brent was reported at **$105.51**, down **$0.41, or 0.39%**, from settlement; November WTI was **$97.43**, down **$0.54, or 0.55%**. (Reuters, Oct 15 20:12 ET)[3]

## U.S. inventories [Energy]

- EIA commercial crude inventories excluding the SPR fell **8.6 million barrels** to **415.6 million barrels** in the week ended October 9; the Reuters analyst poll had expected a **288,000-barrel draw**. (Reuters, Oct 15)[2]
- Total U.S. petroleum inventories including the SPR fell to **696.6 million barrels** for the week ended October 9. (Investing.com, Oct 15 15:42 ET)[5]
- U.S. crude imports fell **791,000 barrels per day** to **6.049 million barrels per day**, while exports fell **195,000 barrels per day** to **4.570 million barrels per day**. (DTN, Oct 15)[4]
- Cushing crude inventories rose **1.86 million barrels** to a five-month high. (Barchart/Yahoo Finance, Oct 15)[7]
- U.S. gasoline inventories rose **3.5 million barrels**, versus expectations for a **911,000-barrel draw**. (Barchart/Yahoo Finance, Oct 15)[7]
- U.S. distillate inventories fell **2.5 million barrels**, versus expectations for a **1.7-million-barrel draw**. (Barchart/Yahoo Finance, Oct 15)[7]
- U.S. crude production fell **2.1% week over week** to **13.679 million barrels per day** in the week ended October 9, from **13.979 million barrels per day** previously. (Barchart/Yahoo Finance, Oct 15)[7]
- Crude inventories were **2.4% below** the seasonal five-year average, gasoline inventories were **4.2% below**, and distillate inventories were **11.7% below** that average. (Barchart/Yahoo Finance, Oct 15)[7]

## OPEC+ [Energy]

- OPEC+ agreed on October 4 to keep **November production quotas unchanged**. (Barchart/Yahoo Finance, Oct 15)[7]

## SPR and emergency supply [Energy]

- The International Energy Agency agreed on October 15 to release **more than 20 million barrels of diesel** and any oil remaining from its emergency-reserve release pledged in March. (Reuters, Oct 16 00:12 ET)[3]

## Sanctions and shipping [Geopolitics / Energy]

- The U.S. administration was considering easing sanctions on some Russian “shadow fleet” tankers to reduce petroleum shipping costs and improve diesel-supply flexibility; the report was attributed to a U.S. official and was **[report]**. (Reuters, Oct 15)[1][2]
- Attacks on shipping near the Strait of Hormuz and Saudi Arabia continued to be reported on October 15, including an attack on Saudi Aramco’s headquarters; the incidents were identified as supply-risk factors. (Reuters, Oct 15)[1][2]
- The IEA’s October report said diesel-market constraints could continue into **2027**. (CNBC, Oct 15)[9]
