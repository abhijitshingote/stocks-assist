## **NVDA** [AI Safety · AI Compute]
- CEO Jensen Huang said Nvidia would hypothetically stop supplying chips to a customer developing unsafe AI software, after first discussing the issue with the customer; he also said AI-safety concerns were “overdone.” Nvidia committed \( \$250 \) million over five years in computing access for UK AI-safety research. (Business Times, Oct 15 07:54 ET)[6]

## Anthropic [AI Safety · Software & SaaS]
- Anthropic selected METR and SecureBio to evaluate its AI-safety practices and said the evaluators would help verify compliance; Accenture was identified as its first partner. (CNBC, Oct 15 16:35 ET)[5]
- New York City Comptroller Mark Levine urged CEO Dario Amodei to pause Anthropic’s IPO until the company addresses AI-safety, governance and development-speed risks, and requested specific disclosures on audits, controls and implementation timelines. [report] (Bloomberg, Oct 15; time not stated)[4][7]

## White House [AI Policy]
- The White House Super Intelligence Task Force was scheduled to hold its first meeting at 16:00 ET on Oct. 15, with more than a dozen Cabinet members and senior officials expected; the group is chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, with Scott Kupor, Emil Michael and FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson as vice chairs. (Politico, Oct 15 15:14 ET)[3]
- The task force’s stated approach centers on applying existing civil and criminal liability laws to AI companies rather than creating new regulations; its goals and responsibilities were expected after the initial meeting. (Politico, Oct 15 15:14 ET)[3]

## TikTok / **BDN** [Internet & Platforms · Antitrust]
- Advocate General Andrea Biondi recommended that the EU Court of Justice dismiss ByteDance’s challenge to TikTok’s designation as a Digital Markets Act gatekeeper, a status carrying obligations intended to limit anti-competitive practices; the opinion was not the final court ruling. (Reuters, Oct 15; time not stated)[1]

## China, United States and EU [AI Policy · Geopolitics]
- China, the United States, the European Union and more than 25 countries signed the Bletchley Declaration, agreeing to cooperate on identifying shared AI risks, developing scientific understanding and creating cross-border mitigation policies. (Economic Times, Oct 15; time not stated)[2]

## Meta [Internet & Platforms · Antitrust]
- The FTC said it would appeal a U.S. district-court ruling that rejected its antitrust case against Meta, maintaining that Meta unlawfully maintained a social-networking monopoly through its acquisitions of Instagram and WhatsApp; the ruling had rejected the FTC’s request for structural relief. (Economic Times, Oct 15; time not stated)[10]
