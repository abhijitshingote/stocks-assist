## Policy scope [HHS/FDA]

- HHS and FDA submitted **“Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides”** to OMB for review; the OMB entry lists **RIN 0910-ZD94**, a **received date of October 14, 2026**, and a **posting/update date of October 15, 2026**. (OMB, Oct. 15)[10]
- The OMB filing disclosed **no peptide list, operating conditions, implementation date, or duration**. (Reuters, Oct. 15; CNBC, Oct. 15 15:36 ET)[1][5]
- Two people familiar with the plan told the Associated Press that the policy would temporarily allow specialty pharmacies to compound selected peptides, including **BPC-157**; this reporting is **[report]** and the proposal remained unconfirmed by HHS and FDA. (Reuters, Oct. 15; CNBC, Oct. 15 15:36 ET)[1][5]
- The reported mechanism is **FDA enforcement discretion**, under which the agency would temporarily refrain from pursuing qualifying pharmacies for producing designated peptides; this would not constitute FDA approval or formal addition to the permitted compounding list. (CNBC, Oct. 15 15:36 ET; Becker’s Hospital Review, Oct. 15)[5][2]
- The FDA’s July 2026 advisory panel recommended easing restrictions on **six of seven** peptides reviewed: **BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax, and Epitalon**; it did not recommend easing restrictions on **emideltide, also called DSIP**. (Frier Levitt, Oct. 14, updated Oct. 15; YourNews, Oct. 15)[7][8]
- The six peptides named in the July advisory recommendations are the reported candidates beyond BPC-157, but the October 15 OMB filing did not confirm that all six were covered by the interim policy. (Frier Levitt, Oct. 14, updated Oct. 15; Reuters, Oct. 15)[7][1]
- The proposed authorization conditions for specialty pharmacies were not disclosed; available reporting described only temporary enforcement discretion for “qualifying” compounding pharmacies. (CNBC, Oct. 15 15:36 ET; Compound Brief, Oct. 15)[5][9]

## OMB review timeline [Regulatory]

- The OMB entry classifies the matter as **Pending EO 12866 Regulatory Review**, with agency/subagency **HHS/FDA**, stage **Notice**, and **no legal deadline**. (OMB, Oct. 15)[10]
- The filing is marked **not economically significant** and lists no statutory review deadline. (OMB, Oct. 15)[10]
- No OMB completion date, publication date, implementation date, or effective date had been announced by **02:03 ET on October 16, 2026**. (OMB, Oct. 15; Reuters, Oct. 15)[10][1]
- A formal rule adding peptides to the FDA’s compounding list would require federal rulemaking and public comment; reporting characterized that process as potentially taking **years**, while a legal analysis estimated **12 to 18 months or longer**. (CNBC, Oct. 15 15:36 ET; Frier Levitt, Oct. 14, updated Oct. 15)[5][7]
- The interim policy could theoretically operate before completion of permanent rulemaking through enforcement discretion, but no final-rule or interim-final-rule effective date had been disclosed. (CNBC, Oct. 15 15:36 ET; Becker’s Hospital Review, Oct. 15)[5][2]
