## **META** [Internet & Platforms]
- The Federal Trade Commission said on October 15 it would appeal a November ruling that rejected its antitrust case alleging Meta illegally maintained a social-networking monopoly through its acquisitions of Instagram and WhatsApp; Meta said the ruling was correct. (The Economic Times, Oct 15, 2026; publish time not provided)[1]

## **OPENAI / ANTHROPIC** [AI Infrastructure]
- The FTC sent civil investigative demands to Anthropic and OpenAI seeking documents on safety incidents, security safeguards, marketing materials, and privacy policies; the demands reportedly exceeded 50 pages each. [report] (Reuters, Oct 15, 2026; publish time not provided)[8]

## **LIVE NATION** [Internet & Platforms]
- Live Nation and Ticketmaster filed a response on October 13 opposing the FTC’s BOTS Act lawsuit, calling it “misguided”; the case alleges Ticketmaster enabled brokers to bypass ticket-purchase limits and resell tickets. (Music Business Worldwide, Oct 15, 2026; publish time not provided)[5]
- U.S. District Judge Maame Ewusi-Mensah Frimpong rejected Live Nation and Ticketmaster’s motion to dismiss on September 28, ruling that the BOTS Act does not preclude liability for ticketing platforms; the factual allegations have not been adjudicated. (Music Business Worldwide, Oct 15, 2026; publish time not provided)[5]

## **REPS & CO.** [Internet & Platforms]
- The DOJ and FTC sued San Diego-based ticket broker REPS & Co. and executives, alleging violations of the BOTS Act through thousands of accounts, VPNs, and concealed IP addresses; the agencies alleged more than 18.6 million tickets for about 83,500 events were bought and resold for more than $120 million in profit. (Head Topics, Oct 16, 2026; publish time not provided)[2]

## **CVS** [Pharma]
- CVS agreed to pay $315 million to resolve DOJ allegations that it illegally filled opioid and other controlled-substance prescriptions since 2013 and sought reimbursement from federal healthcare programs; CVS also agreed to a DEA compliance arrangement covering monitoring, reports, and physician evaluations. (Massachusetts Lawyers Weekly, Oct 15, 2026; updated Oct 16, 2026; publish time not provided)[10]

## **ROCHE / BIOCON** [Pharma]
- Genentech and Biocon Biologics reached a global patent settlement covering Biocon’s proposed Perjeta biosimilar Pebrilzo; Biocon received a licensed U.S. launch window in the third quarter of 2028, subject to regulatory approval. (Patent Lawyer Magazine, Oct 15, 2026; publish time not provided)[3]
- The dispute involved Genentech’s July 2026 New Jersey patent suit alleging infringement of 28 Perjeta-related patents; the claims were voluntarily dismissed without prejudice following the settlement, with no judicial finding on patent validity or infringement. (Patent Lawyer Magazine, Oct 15, 2026; publish time not provided)[3]
