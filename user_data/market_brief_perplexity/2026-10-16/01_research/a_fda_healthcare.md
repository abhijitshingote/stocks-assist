## **EW** Edwards Lifesciences [Pharma / Medical Devices]
- The FDA warned of a potentially high-risk issue involving certain Edwards Commander M delivery systems used with the SAPIEN M3 transcatheter mitral-valve replacement system; Edwards reported three balloon-burst incidents, including one death and one serious injury as of Oct. 5, and asked customers to remove and return affected products. (Reuters, Oct. 15 18:38 ET)[1]

## **HIMS** Hims & Hers Health / Peptide Compounding [Pharma]
- HHS and the FDA submitted an “interim access policy for compounding using certain peptides” to the White House Office of Management and Budget for review; the filing did not disclose the specific policy terms. (Reuters, Oct. 15 17:33 ET)[2]
- Two people familiar with the plan said it would allow specialty pharmacies to temporarily compound certain popular peptides, including BPC-157; the report was marked **[rumor]** because the White House filing did not specify the policy. (CNBC/AP, Oct. 15 15:36 ET)[10]
- FDA advisers had recommended removing restrictions on six of seven peptides reviewed in July, including BPC-157, although FDA staff scientists had concluded that the substances lacked sufficient evidence of safety and effectiveness. (CNBC/AP, Oct. 15 15:36 ET)[10]
- Hims & Hers had previously said it planned to offer peptides if regulations permitted. (Reuters, Oct. 15 17:33 ET)[2]

## **IBRX** ImmunityBio [Pharma]
- The FDA expanded Anktiva’s indication on Oct. 14 to include adults with BCG-unresponsive non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer with high-grade papillary-only tumors; the drug is administered with Bacillus Calmette-Guérin. (FDA, updated Oct. 15)[6]

## **AVXL** Anavex Life Sciences [Pharma]
- Anavex said the FDA cleared its IND application for blarcamesine in Fragile X syndrome, allowing the company to proceed with a U.S. clinical study; blarcamesine is not FDA-approved for any indication. (Anavex, Oct. 15 16:00 ET)[7]

## Rare-Disease Regulation [Pharma]
- The FDA announced a “Rare Disease Framework Initiative” to review disease-specific and fit-for-purpose approaches to rare-disease drug development and regulation; the agency scheduled a public meeting for Oct. 28 and planned to solicit input from patients and experts. (BioPharma Dive, Oct. 15)[8]
