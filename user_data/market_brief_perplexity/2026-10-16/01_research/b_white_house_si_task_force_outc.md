## White House Super Intelligence Task Force `[AI policy]`

- The task force, chaired by National Intelligence Director and AI czar Jay Clayton, held its first meeting at 16:00 ET on Oct. 15, 2026; it adopted a charter during the meeting and has 120 days to produce findings and recommendations for President Donald Trump. (Reuters, Oct. 15; Semafor, Oct. 16)
- The task force commissioned the President’s Council of Advisers on Science and Technology to assess AI risks in 3 areas: cybersecurity and critical-infrastructure protection, biosecurity and dual-use applications, and model control and accountability. (Semafor, Oct. 16)
- The task force created a working group to implement the White House Accord on Super Intelligence, led by Jay Clayton and National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross. (Semafor, Oct. 16)
- Pentagon technology lead Emil Michael was assigned to develop protocols for AI laboratories to report breaches, hacks and other cyber incidents to the government and to coordinate with frontier AI laboratories. (Semafor, Oct. 16)
- The task force created a law-enforcement and consumer-protection coordination committee involving the Justice Department and Federal Trade Commission, led by FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson. (Semafor, Oct. 16)
- The announced priorities include cybersecurity, critical infrastructure, biosecurity, civil liberties, advanced-model standards, consumer protection and the effects of possible AI overregulation. (Semafor, Oct. 16; DW, Oct. 16)
- The task force’s reported charter covers financial services, energy infrastructure, transportation, agriculture and emergency services. (DW, Oct. 16)
- The post-meeting announcement did not identify NVDA, MSFT, GOOGL, META, PLTR or any other individual company as subject to a new directive. (Semafor, Oct. 16)
- The White House statement said the task force would engage frontier-model developers, other super-intelligence companies, consumer-protection groups, civil-liberties advocates, industry stakeholders, and federal and state law-enforcement agencies. (Semafor, Oct. 16)

## AI liability, export controls and compute access `[Policy read-through]`

- Before the meeting, an administration official said the task force would examine using existing liability laws rather than immediately creating new AI-specific regulations; the reported approach concerned accountability when autonomous AI systems cause harm. (Politico, Oct. 15)
- The Oct. 15 post-meeting outputs specified risk assessments, breach-reporting protocols and interagency coordination but did not name a company, chip, model, cloud platform or data-center technology. (Semafor, Oct. 16)
- No post-meeting guidance reported through the 02:03 ET cutoff specified new AI export controls, semiconductor restrictions, compute-allocation rules or mandatory access limits for NVDA, MSFT, GOOGL, META or PLTR. (Semafor, Oct. 16)
- The announced breach-reporting work may include protocols for “breaches, hacks and other cyber issues,” but the post-meeting report said it was not yet clear whether compliance would be legally enforceable. (Semafor, Oct. 16)
- The task force’s reported membership included Vice President JD Vance, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, OSTP Director Michael Kratsios, FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson and Pentagon technology lead Emil Michael; no affected-company list was released. (Semafor, Oct. 16)

## Kevin Warsh `[Fed and AI capital spending]`

- Kevin Warsh was scheduled to participate in a 30-minute IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva fireside chat in Bangkok at 10:30–11:00 Thailand time on Oct. 16, equivalent to 23:30–00:00 ET on Oct. 15–16. (Nation Thailand, Oct. 16; InvestingLive, Oct. 15)
- The available reports before the 02:03 ET cutoff described the event and its expected topics but did not provide verified remarks from Warsh during the speech or a post-speech transcript. (Nation Thailand, Oct. 16; Sedaily, Oct. 15)
- Pre-event reporting said the discussion was expected to address how artificial-intelligence expansion and the energy shock affect central-bank policy and the global economic outlook. (Sedaily, Oct. 15)
- Warsh had previously said AI-related investment and strong investment and consumer spending were reasons current interest rates were not restraining activity; the quoted pre-event report did not establish that he repeated those remarks in Bangkok. (InvestingLive, Oct. 15)
- Companies involved in data centers, utilities, chipmaking and cloud services were expected to raise \( \$300\text{ billion} \) to \( \$570\text{ billion} \) from debt markets in 2026, according to Vanguard, while Warsh had described the resulting financing demand as “competition for capital.” (NBC News, Oct. 15)
- The federal-funds target range was reported at \(3.75\%\)–\(4.00\%\) after a quarter-point September increase, and the next FOMC meeting was scheduled for Oct. 27–28. (InvestingLive, Oct. 15)
- The probability of an October rate increase was reported to have risen from approximately \(20\%\) to \(55\%\) after the September CPI release and later eased to approximately \(40\%\). (Sedaily, Oct. 15)
