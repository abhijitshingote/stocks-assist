## Macro / Calendar `[Macro]`

- The latest FOMC decision was on September 16, 2026: the committee raised the federal-funds target range by 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00%; the vote was unanimous. (Reuters, Oct 15)
- The next FOMC meeting is scheduled for October 27–28, 2026. (Reuters, Oct 15)
- September headline CPI rose 0.6% month over month and 3.7% year over year, versus 3.4% year over year in August; the Reuters consensus was 3.6%. (Reuters, Oct 14, updated Oct 15)
- September core CPI rose 0.3% month over month and 2.6% year over year, versus 2.4% year over year in August; the Reuters consensus was 2.5%. (Reuters, Oct 14, updated Oct 15)
- September gasoline prices rose 9.1% month over month and accounted for more than half of the monthly CPI increase. (Reuters, Oct 15)
- September PPI for final demand rose 1.3% month over month, the largest increase since March 2022, after an upwardly revised 0.5% increase in August. (Reuters, Oct 15)
- Economists estimated September core PCE inflation at 0.35% month over month, which could round to 0.4%, and 3.1% year over year; the estimated range was 0.31%–0.36%. (Reuters, Oct 15)
- The latest reported PCE inflation reading was 3.4% year over year in August, above the Fed’s 2% target. (Reuters, Oct 15)
- Fed funds futures implied roughly a 30% probability of an October 27–28 rate increase after the September PPI report. (Reuters, Oct 15)
- The market had earlier priced roughly even odds of an October hike after the September CPI release, up from about 22% before the report. (Reuters, Oct 14, updated Oct 15)

## Fed policy `[Macro]`

- Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said a “notable deterioration” in public inflation expectations could require the Fed to raise interest rates “more aggressively.” (Reuters, Oct 15)
- San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said, “We are more likely than not to need additional adjustments, but the timing of those adjustments to me is something we’re still deliberating.” (Reuters, Oct 14, republished Oct 15)
- Daly also said, “I don’t feel urgent that we have to decide with a limited set of information,” referring to the October meeting. (Reuters, Oct 14, republished Oct 15)
- Daly said core inflation remained sticky and that she saw no urgency to act at the October 27–28 meeting. (Reuters, Oct 14, republished Oct 15)
- Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman and San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly indicated policymakers could wait for a clearer picture before the October meeting. (Reuters, Oct 15)
- Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s latest cited public stance was described as providing no indication of where he saw policy risks or where rates might need to go. (Reuters, Oct 2, republished Oct 15)

## Economists and investors `[Macro]`

- Raymond James Chief Economist Eugenio Aleman wrote that the CPI report showed “inflation is broadening out,” which “would raise the probability for an increase in rates at the end of the October FOMC meeting.” (Reuters, Oct 14, updated Oct 15)
- Reuters reported that the 10-year Treasury yield fell to about 5.09%, a three-week low, despite the hotter September CPI report. (Reuters, Oct 15)
- Reuters attributed the Treasury rally to investors’ concerns about debt financing for the AI buildout, an AI-stock selloff, and perceived risks to growth; no named economist or investor quote was provided in the available report. (Reuters, Oct 15)
- Two-year Treasury yields rose 12 basis points on the September CPI release, while 10-year yields rose 7 basis points; the spread between them narrowed by 5 basis points. (Reuters, Oct 14, republished Oct 15)
- Reuters reported that the market initially raised the implied probability of an October hike to about 50% after CPI, before subsequent Fed commentary reduced the probability to roughly 30% after PPI. (Reuters, Oct 15)
