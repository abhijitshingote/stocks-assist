## **NVDA** / **AMD** / **AVGO** / **INTC** / **MU** / **SNDK** — Semiconductors
- U.S. semiconductor stocks were on track to lose about **$2 trillion** in market value on October 15 after six sessions of selling; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index was down **3.3%** at the time of the report. The report attributed the decline to increased expectations for higher interest rates and concerns about the cost of AI infrastructure. (Reuters, Oct 15; publish time not stated)[1]
- **Nvidia (NVDA)** fell about **2%**, **Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)** about **4%**, **Micron (MU)** about **4%**, and **SanDisk (SNDK)** about **6%** in premarket trading on October 15; **Broadcom (AVGO)** and **Intel (INTC)** also declined, with percentage moves not specified. (Yahoo Finance, Oct 15 03:44 ET)[6]
- **SanDisk (SNDK)** later fell **6%** during the October 15 session, after losing **5%** on October 14. (Yahoo Finance, Oct 15; publish time not stated)[10]

## **TSM** — Fab & Foundry
- Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. reported third-quarter net profit of **NT$763.1 billion ($23.59 billion)**, up **69% year over year**, and forecast fourth-quarter revenue of up to **$54.3 billion**. The report said the quarterly profit exceeded market forecasts; consensus EPS and revenue figures were not provided. (Reuters, Oct 15; publish time not stated)[2][6]
- **TSM** fell about **2%** in Asian trading and declined **2%** in the U.S. October 15 session despite the quarterly results. (Reuters, Oct 15; publish time not stated; Yahoo Finance, Oct 15; publish time not stated)[2][10]

## **SOX / Semiconductor sector** — Macro / Calendar
- The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index ended October 15 down nearly **1%**, after being down as much as **2%** during the session. (Reuters, Oct 15; publish time not stated)[4]
- Reuters reported that the six-session selloff had erased approximately **$1.64 trillion** in U.S. semiconductor-stock market value before the additional October 15 decline. (Reuters, Oct 15; publish time not stated)[1]
- The October 15 selloff was linked in the reports to concerns about AI infrastructure spending, debt financing for data centers, and expectations for additional Federal Reserve interest-rate increases. (Reuters, Oct 15; publish time not stated)[1][3]
