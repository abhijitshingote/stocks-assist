### 🧭 Thematic / Sector Weakness

#### 🔻 AI / Semiconductor Rout → `Technology` + `Communication Services`

**MU** (-14.2%), **MRVL** (-13.2%), **ARM** (-12.9%), **SNDK** (-12.5%), **GLW** (-11.5%), **WDC** (-11.1%), **QCOM** (-11.0%), **AMD** (-10.9%), **INTC** (-10.7%), **ORCL** (-10.7%), **LRCX** (-10.2%), **AMAT** (-9.7%), **KLAC** (-9.2%), **STX** (-9.0%), **SMCI** (-12.0%), **NBIS** (-13.9%)

- Broadcom (AVGO) reiterated its FY2027 AI semiconductor revenue target of >$100B instead of raising it, triggering a violent sector‑wide profit‑taking cascade.
- The May nonfarm payrolls print surged to 172,000 (est. 85,000), pushing 2‑year Treasury yields above 4.15% and cementing expectations for a Fed rate hike by year‑end.
- Micron’s “Why Is Micron Stock Falling Friday?” article explicitly ties the decline to Broadcom’s unchanged AI outlook and rate‑hike jitters.
- Marvell fell after an explosive multi‑day rally fueled by NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang’s “next trillion‑dollar company” comment at Computex; the Broadcom shock sparked aggressive profit‑taking.
- ARM’s article cites Broadcom’s guidance as the primary catalyst, with Mizuho raising its PT to $500 but momentum overstretched.
- SanDisk, Western Digital, and Seagate were caught in the memory/storage downdraft despite Goldman Sachs’ positive view on memory supply tightness.
- QCOM slid despite JPMorgan’s upbeat preview of its June 24 Investor Day, as the Broadcom‑induced selloff overwhelmed stock‑specific narratives.
- Intel and AMD both fell in sympathy; Intel’s article notes profit‑taking after a 459% 12‑month run, while AMD’s notes the quantum‑AI collaboration with JPMorgan but the tape overwhelmed.
- Oracle dropped as BNP Paribas flagged concerns over debt‑funded AI capex potentially hitting $80‑100B in FY27, adding to AI infrastructure jitters.
- Equipment names (LRCX, AMAT, KLAC) were slammed alongside the SOX index, with no stock‑specific news.
- SMCI, a server builder, was hit by the same AI hardware unwind; its article references the Broadcom trigger.
- Nebius slid due to AI infrastructure cooling; a separate catalyst was NVIDIA director Mark Stevens’ $221M share sale, but the sector theme was dominant.

#### 🚀 Space Stocks Collapse → `Technology` (Communication Equipment) + `Industrials` (Aerospace & Defense)

**ASTS** (-14.5%), **PL** (-26.2%)

- S&P Global ruled out changes to index eligibility rules, dashing hopes that SpaceX could quickly join the S&P 500 post‑IPO, removing a passive‑flow catalyst for the entire sector.
- AST SpaceMobile fell 10.4% intraday, as did Rocket Lab, Redwire, and others; the “Space Stocks Bleed” article explicitly names ASTS among the casualties.
- Planet Labs, a satellite‑imaging company, was doubly hit: it announced a $1.5B at‑the‑market equity offering, sparking severe dilution fears, while also suffering from the broader space‑sector rout that AXTs and other space names experienced.

#### ☀️ Clean Energy / Solar Pressure → `Energy` (Solar)

**FSLR** (-13.6%)

- The “Nasdaq 100 Plunges Over 3%…” article notes First Solar fell ~11% as part of a clean‑energy rout tied to threatened rollback of solar tax credits and the surge in Treasury yields.

### 🎯 Idiosyncratic Movers

**NOK** (–13.6%, Large) → Idiosyncratic  
- Nokia issued €500M in senior unsecured notes due 2032, using proceeds to refinance existing debt. Combined with profit‑taking after a 208% 12‑month rally, the stock fell sharply. Broader tech weakness added to the move.

**IONQ** (–13.2%, Large) → Idiosyncratic  
- High‑beta quantum computing name faded after a fast run‑up on CHIPS Act grant speculation. Quantinuum’s IPO opened 13% above its $60 offer price but then faded, cooling enthusiasm across quantum peers.

**CRCL** (–12.0%, Large) → Idiosyncratic  
- Mizuho maintained a Neutral rating but slashed its price target from $135 to $85. The stock, a crypto‑exposed financial services firm, also suffered from the sharp decline in Bitcoin (BTC) to $59,163, its lowest since 2024.

**BE** (–11.7%, Large) → Idiosyncratic  
- Bloom Energy gave back some of its recent tariff‑relief gains as traders reassessed the impact of the new 15% tariff rate on steel/aluminum derivatives and a broader risk‑off tone. No fresh company‑specific negative.
