- The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) sank 3.4% on Friday as investors rotated out of chipmakers and AI‑hardware names into software and defensives (IGV +3.2%).  
- Selling was “deepening across the complex” and extended pressure that had been building since Broadcom’s recent AI‑revenue guidance disappointed (Benzinga Pro).  
- Sharpest pain was in memory and storage: WDC dropped over 10% after Micron’s blowout quarter earlier in the week failed to sustain gains; profit‑taking hit STX, SNDK, and MU.  
- Broader AI‑infra supply chain and equipment names (ANET, KLAC, ENTG, FORM) followed the unwind.

### 🎯 Idiosyncratic Movers

**ON** (-23.2%, Large cap) → Idiosyncratic  
- ON Semiconductor announced an all‑stock acquisition of Synaptics in a ~$7 bn enterprise‑value deal.  
- ON shares tumbled as the fixed 1.35‑share‑for‑1 exchange ratio implied dilution; the transaction is expected to close in mid‑2027 and ON reaffirmed its outlook, but the stock was penalized sharply.  

**MS** (-4.1%, Mega cap) → Idiosyncratic  
- Morgan Stanley’s $7 bn North Haven Private Income Fund fulfilled only 43% of second‑quarter redemption requests after investor demands reached ~11.6% of outstanding units.  
- The partial gating, following similar moves by Apollo and Blackstone, underscored ongoing liquidity strains in private credit.  

**VC** (-10.3%, Mid & small cap) → Idiosyncratic

- **Memory cycle durability**: Micron’s strong quarter was immediately sold; next memory read‑across comes from **Western Digital** earnings expected **July 29**.  
- **Synaptics integration**: ON Semiconductor’s $7 bn deal won’t close until **mid‑2027**; regulatory and shareholder approvals are pending, and the $235 mn/$320 mn termination fees keep risk elevated.  
- **Private‑credit liquidity**: Morgan Stanley’s gating follows similar steps from Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock; continued heavy redemption requests could pressure net asset values and investor sentiment.
