### 🧭 Thematic / Sector Weakness

**Semiconductor Rout ( Memory & AI Chips )** → `Technology`

- **SNDK** (-14.1%), **MU** (-11.5%), **ARM** (-9.9%), **VRT** (-9.7%), **MRVL** (-8.7%), **ADI** (-8.4%), **TXN** (-8.3%), **WDC** (-8.1%), **KLAC** (-8.0%), **GLW** (-6.8%), **AMAT** (-6.4%), **ANET** (-6.4%), **ETN** (-6.3%), **TSEM** (-10.2%), **CRDO** (-9.8%), **COHR** (-9.8%), **ON** (-9.5%), **MCHP** (-8.7%), **STM** (-8.4%), **ALAB** (-7.7%), **ENTG** (-7.7%), **LITE** (-7.7%), **AXTI** (-15.4%), **CAMT** (-12.9%), **AAOI** (-12.9%).
- South Korea’s KOSPI plunged ~10% Tuesday, with Samsung and SK Hynix each down >12%, triggering circuit breakers and spilling into US chip ETFs (SMH -8%, SOXX -8%).
- The rout was fueled by an unconfirmed Korean report that SK Hynix may shift HBM capacity toward general‑purpose DRAM, stoking fears of slowing AI memory demand.
- Micron’s earnings (due after June 24 close) added speculative pressure; options priced a ~14% implied move, and Polymarket showed 96% odds of an EPS beat but an 85% chance of hitting $1,050 this week.
- Wedbush’s Dan Ives called the selloff a “gut check moment” and said the AI revolution is only in the “third inning.” Bank of America raised its Micron PT to $1,500, citing memory supply tightness into 2028.
- Futurum Group CEO Daniel Newman described memory as “the GPU of three years ago,” stating the era of memory as a commodity is over.
- The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) shed ~6%; the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) fell ~8%. Software names held firm, showing the selloff was chip‑specific.

### 🎯 Idiosyncratic Movers

- COO Jeremy Kinch departed effective immediately; President/CEO will assume most COO duties while a search is conducted.  
- FY26 guidance was slashed: adjusted EPS lowered from $4.80‑$5.00 to $2.05‑$2.60; Renewables revenue now seen at ~$2.1B (previously ~$3.0B) due to cost overruns on six legacy projects.  
- KeyBanc downgraded to Sector Weight; Mizuho cut PT to $117; Wells Fargo lowered to $85. Guggenheim kept Buy/$162 PT.
