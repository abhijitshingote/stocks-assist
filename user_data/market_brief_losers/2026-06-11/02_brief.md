### 🧭 Thematic / Sector Weakness

**Semiconductor selloff** → `Technology`

- Names: **QCOM** (-5.6%), **AVGO** (-4.7%), **ARM** (-3.9%), **TSM** (-3.8%)
- Broadcom’s fiscal Q2 report on June 4 maintained its >$100B AI semiconductor revenue forecast for fiscal 2027 rather than raising it, which triggered profit‑taking across premium-valued AI stocks (AVGO article).
- Qualcomm slid amid a “broader semiconductor correction” after a roller‑coaster stretch that included a spike on unverified social media footage; JPMorgan raised its price target to $265 but kept Neutral (QCOM article).
- Arm Holdings fell as investors “took profits across the semiconductor sector” and reacted to macro pressures, with the stock still up 190% YTD (ARM article).
- TSMC dropped in a “broader risk‑off move” despite reporting May revenue up 30.1% YoY; pre‑market weakness was driven by sentiment rather than company‑specific news (TSM article).
- The common thread is a cooling of AI‑chip sentiment following Broadcom’s unchanged outlook, compounded by a risk‑off day.

**Travel & Leisure hit by oil surge** → `Consumer Cyclical` / `Industrials (Airlines)`

- Names: **CCL** (-6.9%), **ULCC** (-9.8%)
- Oil prices jumped after new U.S. strikes in the Strait of Hormuz reignited supply concerns; West Texas Intermediate climbed 3.3% to ~$91.07/bbl (CCL, CAT articles).
- Carnival fell as “cruise operators came under pressure from rising oil prices and a broader risk‑off move,” with elevated fuel costs threatening operating expenses (CCL article).
- The IATA sliced its global airline profit outlook, warning that the Hormuz closure has roughly doubled jet‑fuel prices, pushed crack spreads to a record $80/bbl, and may limit industry margins to 2%—the weakest since COVID (ULCC article).
- Both names are directly exposed to fuel costs and share the same geopolitical/oil‑supply catalyst.

### 🎯 Idiosyncratic Movers

**SMCI** (-25.3%, Large cap) → Idiosyncratic  
- Super Micro announced a $7B equity and equity‑linked financing package ($5B concurrent public offerings of common stock and mandatory convertible preferred depositary shares, plus a $2B at‑the‑market program).  
- Proceeds are intended to fund component purchases for ~$39B in AI‑server orders from >20 customers.  
- Shares plunged on dilution fears; the Defiance 2X Short ETF (SMCZ) surged ~40% on the day (SMCI articles).

**CAT** (-6.2%, Mega cap) → Idiosyncratic (linked to sector but no overlapping article among other industrials)  
- The decline was driven by rising oil prices after U.S. military actions in the Strait of Hormuz, heightening supply concerns and inflation fears.  
- Higher energy costs raised the risk that rates stay elevated, weighing on demand for heavy equipment (CAT article).  
- Separately, Caterpillar increased its quarterly dividend from $1.41 to $1.63/share; the dividend move did not offset the macro selloff.

**BE** (-9.7%, Large cap) → Idiosyncratic  
- Bloom Energy faced valuation concerns after Morningstar recently called it the most overvalued stock in its coverage, pegging fair value at ~$70 vs. a price >$250.  
- The stock had rallied ~1,300% over the past year, and the pullback was amplified by a broader risk‑off tone. Tariff adjustments (steel/aluminum derivatives cut to 15% for certain imports) provided only mixed relief (BE article).

**CGEM** (-10.7%, Mid cap) → Idiosyncratic  
- Cullinan Therapeutics reported clinical data for its CD19 and BCMA T‑cell engagers, showing remissions in SLE and RA, and outlined global development plans.  
- Despite the positive‑sounding data, the stock fell sharply in a sell‑the‑news reaction (CGEM article).

**UEC** (-9.9%, Mid cap) → Idiosyncratic  
- Uranium Energy reported a wider fiscal Q3 loss of $0.11 per share (vs. -$0.07 a year ago) on no revenue.  
- Production costs rose to $54.61/lb due to timing of regulatory approvals and higher state taxes.  
- The company is holding ~1.5M lbs of uranium inventory, betting on higher future spot prices (UEC articles).
