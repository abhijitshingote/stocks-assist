### Nvidia (NVDA) – Blowout Print, CPU Pivot, Sovereign AI, But Profit‑Taking

NVDA underperformed on the day (-2.27% to $215.23), giving back part of a post‑earnings surge even as fundamentals stayed exceptionally strong.

Fundamentally, NVDA just delivered another “crushing” quarter. Fiscal Q1 revenue came in around **$81–81.62 billion**, up roughly **85% Y/Y** and ahead of Street expectations (~$78.8–79.2 billion). Data‑center revenue was **$75.2 billion** (about **92%** of total), and adjusted EPS of **$1.87** topped estimates (~$1.76–1.77). Management guided Q2 above expectations while maintaining non‑GAAP gross margins around **75%**, with similar margin guidance for the current quarter. The company also sharply stepped up capital return: the board approved an **$80 billion** buyback authorization and boosted the quarterly dividend from **$0.01 to $0.25** (annual **$1.00**), a 25x increase that brings NVDA’s annual payout closer to Apple’s **$1.08**.

### AMD (AMD) – CPUs Back To Center Stage, Taiwan Capex, Sovereign AI

AMD was a major outperformer Friday, rising **+4.98% to $466.49**, materially ahead of SOXX’s +2.33%. The move built on an increasingly CPU‑centric AI narrative and aggressive capacity plans.

CEO Lisa Su emphasized that AI inferencing and “agentic AI” workloads are driving a much stronger‑than‑expected surge in CPU demand, putting CPUs back at the center of data‑center compute after a GPU‑dominated period. AMD plans to invest **over $10 billion** across Taiwan’s AI supply chain to expand advanced manufacturing and packaging, targeting substrates, advanced packaging and rack‑scale AI systems with co‑investment from partners including **ASE, SPIL, PTI, Wiwynn, Wistron, Inventec, Unimicron, Nan Ya PCB, and Kinsus**. Su confirmed AMD has begun ramping **Venice** CPUs on **TSMC’s 2nm** process and projected the AI infrastructure market could exceed **$1 trillion** over the next **3–4 years**, with the CPU market potentially growing **>35% annually** over the next five years. She highlighted AMD’s full AI stack across **CPUs, GPUs, and ASICs** and said China still accounts for around **20%** of AMD revenue, with the company planning to continue serving Chinese customers within U.S. export rules. AMD was noted as trading **+2.60% pre‑market at $461.26** and closing near its **$469.21** 52‑week high.

### Intel (INTC) – AI Inference M&A, CPU Rival To NVDA, ETF/Rotation Tailwinds

INTC gained **+1.26% to $119.57**, participating in the AI compute bid while lagging AMD’s move. Strategically, Intel is pushing deeper into AI inference through M&A: Bloomberg‑sourced reporting says INTC is exploring a potential acquisition of AI chip startup **Tenstorrent**, which makes inference‑focused AI chips, with **QCOM** also in discussions. Tenstorrent is working with investment banks and could be valued at **over $5 billion** in a deal, with the report casting this as part of a broader race among chipmakers to secure AI technology and talent to compete with NVDA and AMD.
