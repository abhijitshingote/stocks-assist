The name remains central to AI infrastructure, but Friday’s move showed investors rotating into secondary and tertiary beneficiaries.

Intel also traded higher at **+1.26%** as the market continued to rotate toward CPU exposure and other AI hardware beneficiaries.

QCOM was the standout mover, surging **+12.48%** with the broader AI hardware rally. TXN also jumped **+4.20%** after a bullish analyst call, while ADI gained **+3.32%** on the back of its recent beat and strong analog momentum. ARM added **+3.92%** as the AI/semis bid broadened.

In equipment, **ASML +2.85%**, **KLAC +2.34%**, **LRCX +1.85%**, and **AMAT +0.92%** all participated in the sector strength, reflecting continued enthusiasm around advanced-node and AI build-out spending.

MU edged up **+0.65%** even as the market digested a big AI-memory narrative. The stock remains tied to tight DRAM/HBM supply and U.S. memory expansion headlines, but Friday’s move was modest relative to the broader semi rally.

MRVL continued to grind higher, finishing **+2.71%**. The stock remains a major AI-custom-silicon beneficiary, with sell-side targets moving up into earnings and the name trading near highs.

TSM slipped **-0.95%** despite ongoing optimism around advanced-node manufacturing and broader AI-capex tailwinds. AVGO also underperformed slightly at **-0.18%**.

Overall, the session reinforced a familiar theme: AI infra capex is still the dominant driver, but the market is increasingly rewarding CPU, analog, equipment, memory, and custom-silicon names alongside the GPU leaders.
