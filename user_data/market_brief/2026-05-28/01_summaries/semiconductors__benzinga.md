# Semiconductors — benzinga

_kind: sector · elapsed: 188.2s_

Semiconductors traded **mixed-to-weak** against a flat broader tape on Wednesday, May 27: **SPY -0.01%, QQQ -0.11%, XLK -0.43%, IGV -1.04%, SOXX -1.07%**, with notable dispersion across AI GPUs/CPUs, memory, foundry, semicap equipment, analog and RF/power.

Within large-cap semis, **memory and foundry outperformed** while several AI CPU/GPU and semicap names lagged. **Micron (MU +0.95%, close $920.78)** continued to grind higher after a prior ~19% spike and its break into the **$1T+ market‑cap club**, trading around 38% above its 20‑day SMA and 190.5% above its 200‑day SMA and sitting near fresh 52‑week highs.[52802223][52802223] MU is being framed as a core **AI‑memory and DRAM/HBM cycle winner**, with Counterpoint citing record **Q1 2026 DRAM revenue of $97B (+80% QoQ, +260% YoY)** and expecting DRAM prices to rise another ~50% QoQ in Q2, driving **>300% DRAM market growth in 2026** as Samsung and Micron aggressively add capacity.[52803875][52803875] Street commentary highlights MU’s **~9–10x forward P/E** even after an **~800–859% 1‑year gain** and a market cap around **$1.01–1.03T**, with UBS lifting its price target to **$1,625** (Street‑high) and other recent raises from Citi and Melius to **$840** and **$1,100**.[52831386][52802223][52802223] Over the past five years MU has delivered **61.64% average annual returns**, turning a $100 investment into **$1,103.28**, and Goldman now estimates **NVDA and MU could provide roughly one‑third of S&P 500 EPS growth** this year, positioning MU as a key index‑level AI driver alongside Nvidia.[52828349][52830881] At the same time, Morningstar’s William Kerwin warns that capacity additions at MU, Samsung, SK Hynix and Chinese peers could set up **oversupply starting in late 2027–2028**, highlighting longer‑term cyclicality risk despite the current AI‑memory boom.[52831386]

**TSMC (TSM +2.37%, close $423.18)** outperformed as the **foundry epicenter of AI** trade remained in focus.[52799950] Jensen Huang reiterated that **Taiwan is the “epicentre of the AI revolution”** and said **Nvidia plans to raise its annual spend in Taiwan from $10–15B a few years ago to ~$100B now and toward ~$150B**, spanning procurement, manufacturing partnerships and infrastructure around partners like TSMC, Foxconn, Wistron and Quanta.[52799950][52804711] TSMC’s CEO has pledged a **“30%-plus” incentive bump** amid surging AI‑driven profits, and the stock has joined the **$1T market‑cap club** alongside other Asian AI bellwethers.[52801675][52800917] Broader foundry strength was supported by **UMC’s update on its 12nm joint program with Intel**, which remains on track with certification at Intel’s Arizona site and **mass production targeted for 2027**, reinforcing secular demand for advanced nodes and specialty processes tied to AI, auto, 5G and advanced packaging.[52800607][52800607]

**Nvidia (NVDA -0.59%, close $212.35)** dipped modestly despite being central to nearly all of these AI‑infra narratives. Beyond the Taiwan capex ramp, Huang confirmed plans to build a **Taiwan HQ for ~4,000 engineers**, breaking ground this year with operations targeted by 2030 and highlighting a shift in NVDA’s geographic revenue mix: **China + Hong Kong revenue has declined while Taiwan revenue rose >50% YoY** in the latest quarter.[52799950][52808834] Separately, NVDA’s board approved a **25x dividend increase from $0.01 to $0.25 per share**, lifting CEO Jensen Huang’s annual dividend income by roughly **$810M** based on his ~844M‑share stake, while still keeping the yield below 1%.[52812431] Sell‑side support remained firm, with **Tigress Financial maintaining a Strong Buy and raising its NVDA PT to $425 from $360**, and macro pieces from BlackRock and Goldman flagging AI chips—especially **NVDA and MU**—as primary engines of S&P 500 earnings growth and as a potential source of persistent inflationary pressure given the **“$800B AI binge”** in capex.[52811875][52814176][52805555] Another Benzinga piece noted that semis now constitute **~18% of S&P 500 market cap** and have driven **~70% of the index’s YTD gains**, with the **PHLX Semiconductor Index up ~79% YTD and 162.7% over 12 months**, underscoring how AI chips dominate index‑level performance.[52830881]

Other **GPU/CPU complex** names were weaker. **AMD -1.60% (close $495.55)** pulled back after a **148% quarter‑to‑date surge**, a run on track to be its **best quarter since 1Q 1975**, even as multiple brokers (Evercore, KeyBanc, Bernstein, Mizuho, Wells Fargo, Citi, BofA) continued to raise targets and emphasize AMD’s expanding AI footprint across accelerators, CPUs, networking and custom silicon.[52811968][52806131] AMD is trading around **168x P/E** with a **Buy** consensus and average PT near **$460**, ahead of an expected **August 4, 2026** earnings print where Street models **EPS $1.55 vs $0.48 YoY** and **revenue $11.28B vs $7.68B YoY**.[52806131] On the infrastructure front, **OneQode plans to deploy AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs and eventually the Helios rack‑scale platform across Europe and APAC**, leveraging ROCm for large‑scale AI training and inference as part of a **110MW AI‑infrastructure project with Bitzero in Norway**, while AMD separately pledged **>$10B of investment in Taiwan** to support Helios and next‑gen AI builds in 2H 2026.[52806131][52808834] **Intel (INTC -2.29%, close $120.48)** also retraced despite strong YTD gains (over 200% in some QQQ framing) and structural U.S. policy support: the **U.S. government’s stake in Intel—initially $8.9B—has reportedly grown to >$50B** as of mid‑May, and Intel is a key partner in UMC’s 12nm program, yet the stock traded with sector beta rather than headlines.[52829639][52825718][52800607] A separate Reuters‑based note added that **ByteDance is developing custom CPUs** to support AI workloads, evaluating **Arm and RISC‑V architectures** and positioning these chips as complements to NVDA GPUs in inference, a move that underscores both the demand for data‑center CPUs and the long‑run risk of **in‑house silicon** to x86 incumbents.[52830571]

**Qualcomm (QCOM -7.80%, close $228.12)** was the day’s biggest laggard in the group. One Benzinga piece tied the weakness to **profit‑taking after a sharp AI‑driven rally**, even as Bloomberg reported a **ByteDance deal to supply AI data‑center ASICs**, reinforcing Qualcomm’s data‑center AI ambitions.[52816461] Another wrap, however, simply framed the drop as part of a broader AI‑hardware beta shake‑out amid macro worries about AI capex and inflation, without name‑specific catalysts in that packet.[52814176] **Arm (ARM -4.79%, close $303.96)** also sold off with no incremental company news, trading as a high‑beta AI‑IP proxy in a session where investors increasingly scrutinized the inflationary implications of hyperscaler AI build‑outs that BlackRock argued may keep interest rates elevated.[52814176]  

On the **AI networking and custom‑silicon side**, **Marvell (MRVL -3.31%, close $200.76)** was weak during the regular session despite delivering a **record Q1 FY27** and issuing one of the strongest multi‑year AI guides in semis after the bell. MRVL had run into the print (prior close **+6.08% to $208.26** and **+6.17% premarket to $221.12**) on expectations of **$0.75 EPS on $2.40B revenue** and bullish AI‑infrastructure positioning.[52800359] The company reported **Q1 revenue $2.418B** (vs $2.404B est., **+28% YoY, +9% QoQ**) and **non‑GAAP EPS $0.80** (vs $0.79 est., **+29% YoY**), with **data center revenue a record $1.83B (76% of total, +27% YoY, +11% QoQ)** and non‑GAAP gross margin at **58.9%**.[52829008][52827733][52827733] Management guided **Q2 revenue to $2.7B ±5%**, implying **+12% QoQ and +35% YoY** at the midpoint, with data center expected to grow **mid‑ to high‑teens sequentially** and **mid‑40% YoY**, and set **Q2 non‑GAAP EPS guidance at $0.88–0.98** vs $0.90 consensus and revenue of **$2.565–2.835B** vs ~$2.6B est.[52827733][52824374][52824993] On the call and in subsequent headlines, CEO Matt Murphy said MRVL is seeing **“exceptional AI‑related bookings”** and **“significantly raising” its revenue outlook for FY27 and FY28**, now expecting **overall FY27 revenue to grow ~40% YoY to nearly $11.5B** and **FY28 to reach ~$16.5B (~45% growth)**, both **>$0.5–1.5B above** prior views.[52824993][52826602][52826627]

The detailed bridge underscores how **AI networking, optics and custom silicon** are becoming core bottlenecks—and profit pools—beneath GPUs. In **interconnect**, MRVL lifted its FY27 revenue outlook from ~50% to **“more than 70%” YoY**, citing the **1.6T PAM4 ramp this year** and another substantial step up in FY28.[52827733][52826875] Management expects its **TIA/driver analog business to exceed a $1B annualized run‑rate in the next few quarters**, while **scale‑out switch revenue is projected to more than double to >$600M in FY27 and to track to >$1B annualized in FY28**.[52827733] In **DCI modules**, Marvell ships to **all five major U.S. hyperscalers** and targets **$1B annualized DCI revenue in FY28**, about **2x FY26’s ~$500M**, and sees future “scale‑across” AI networks where bandwidth between data centers could be **>10x current front‑end DCI needs**, supported by **2nm coherent DSPs** sampling this year and roadmaps extending to **3.2T and beyond**.[52827733][52827733] On **custom silicon**, MRVL is on track for **>20% YoY custom revenue growth in FY27** and expects custom revenue to **“more than double” in FY28**, reiterating a **> $10B FY29 custom revenue target** tied to roughly 20% share of a **$55B custom silicon TAM**, backed by **>10 XPU‑attach programs** and a new Tier‑1 XPU win across all major U.S. hyperscalers.[52827733][52827183][52827506]  

Strategically, Marvell deepened its role in the **Nvidia ecosystem** and AI networking stack. It announced an **expanded partnership with NVDA built on three pillars**:  
- **Optics and silicon photonics** – extending MRVL’s role as a key DSP/TIA/driver supplier into **plasmonics‑based silicon photonics**, helped by the **Polariton** acquisition, which brings **>1 THz modulator bandwidth (up to 10x current approaches)** for future 1.6T/3.2T+ modules.[52827733][52827733]  
- **NVLink Fusion** – enabling Marvell custom chips and networking silicon to **interface directly with Nvidia infrastructure**, letting hyperscalers mix custom and merchant solutions while using MRVL as the “bridge” between architectures.[52827733]  
- **AI‑RAN** – integrating **Octeon base‑station processors** with NVDA GPUs so telcos can run **5G/6G and AI workloads on the same platform**, creating a path for AI‑driven radio access networks.[52827733]  

MRVL also closed the **Polariton** deal and acquired **Celestial AI and XCON**, integrating Celestial’s **photonic fabric and low‑power analog SerDes** for next‑gen XPU scale‑up networks already **selected by a Tier‑1 hyperscaler**, and now expects **FY28 scale‑up optics revenue to be >2x its prior ~$150M outlook** that had assumed only Celestial.[52827733] Management highlighted **CXL and NIC XPU‑attach** as a major growth driver, with **>10 attach programs** and demand exceeding prior forecasts, particularly for **CXL memory attach and NICs used for inference key‑value caching**, implying incremental opportunity for **memory vendors (e.g., MU) and server CPU/DPU/controller suppliers** across the stack.[52827733] Financially, MRVL exited the quarter with **GAAP gross margin 52.1%, non‑GAAP 58.9%, non‑GAAP operating margin 35%**, record **$639M operating cash flow**, **$1.4B inventory (flat QoQ)**, **$4.96B total debt** (gross leverage 1.44x, net 0.32x), and returned capital via **$200M in buybacks and $54M in dividends**.[52827733] To secure AI supply, the company plans roughly **$1B of prepayments to suppliers in FY27**, starting Q2, funded from its balance sheet and cash flow and applied against future purchases, while targeting the **upper end of its 38–40% operating‑margin model by late FY28** with opex growth well below revenue.[52827733]

This **MRVL guide and NVDA/AMD/TSM capex commentary** together underscore the same structural story: **AI infrastructure capex is stepping down the stack** from GPUs into memory (MU and peers), optical interconnects and switching (MRVL), and advanced foundry and packaging capacity (TSM, INTC/UMC), with ripple effects for **semicap equipment and analog/RF/power**.
