# Software & SaaS — benzinga

_kind: sector · elapsed: 115.6s_

Software and SaaS traded **mixed-to-weak** on Wednesday, May 27, with broad tech indices slightly lower (**SPY -0.01%**, **QQQ -0.11%**, **SOXX -1.07%**, **IGV -1.04%**, **XLK -0.43%**) and most large-cap application, data, and security names in the red, even as a handful of workflow and tax names outperformed.[52831354]

Pressure was broad-based across large-cap software: **MSFT -0.53% (close $413.39)**, **ORCL -0.78% ($190.43)**, **CRM -0.70% ($178.94)**, **SNOW -1.71% ($175.17)**, **DDOG -1.00% ($222.29)**, **MDB -3.66% ($295.98)**, **ESTC -0.47% ($54.31)**, **ADBE -1.37% ($238.50)**, and security leaders **PANW -3.97% ($248.53)**, **CRWD -4.35% ($645.35)**, **ZS -32.99% ($126.90)** all finished lower, while **NOW +1.89% ($102.76)**, **TEAM +4.82% ($88.98)**, and **INTU +1.11% ($307.38)** bucked the trend.[52831354]

Cybersecurity was the focal point of the downside move: **Zscaler (ZS)** cratered **-32.99%** after reporting a solid FQ3 beat but issuing FQ4 revenue guidance of **$875–$878M**, with the midpoint below the **$878.53M** consensus and outlining a more conservative FY27 framework of **16–17%** implied ARR and revenue growth versus Street expectations of **18.4%/19.5%**, alongside commentary on sales-leadership turnover, softer EMEA execution, and execution risk around integrated SecOps adoption.[52821082][52814578] ZS’ numbers were otherwise strong, with FQ3 revenue **$850.5M vs $835.6M** consensus, ARR **$3.53B (+25% YoY)**, non‑GAAP EPS **$1.08**, non‑GAAP operating margin **23% (+140 bps YoY)**, net new ARR **$166M** (including **$13M** from Red Canary vs **$6M** guided), 4,003 customers with >**$100K** ARR (+19% YoY), 748 with >**$1M** ARR (+18% YoY), and RPO up **30%** to about **$6.5B**.[52814578] Shares were cited **-21.4% to $145.00 pre‑market** and tumbled intraday before closing at **$126.90**, prompting a sweeping wave of target cuts from the sell side (Wedbush, Cantor, JPM, Macquarie, Scotiabank, Stifel, Canaccord, Rosenblatt, Mizuho, Piper Sandler, RBC, among others) but mostly with positive ratings maintained.[52803370][52814578] The ZS guide reset drove a sector‑wide derisking, consistent with sharp weakness in **PANW -3.97%** and **CRWD -4.35%**, even as firms like Wedbush and Benchmark reiterated bullish medium‑term views on Palo Alto Networks and raised targets (e.g., Wedbush **$225 → $300**, Benchmark **$200 → $270**).[52806158][52809853][52821535]

**CrowdStrike (CRWD)** slid **-4.35%** despite positive company-specific news: the stock pulled back after a strong run as traders faded a new German go‑to‑market partnership with **SVA System Vertrieb Alexander GmbH** to distribute its AI-native Falcon platform to public-sector, enterprise, and mid-market customers via **AWS Marketplace, STACKIT, and Google Cloud Marketplace**, and de‑risked ahead of the **June 3** earnings print.[52806715] Technicals were extended (RSI **86.84**, trading **18.3%** above its 20‑day SMA and **38.6%** above its 50‑day SMA), and expectations remained elevated with Street looking for **$0.88 EPS vs $0.73** a year ago and **$1.36B revenue vs $1.10B** YoY, supported by a Buy‑skewed rating stack and recent PT hikes from BTIG (**to $764**), Wells Fargo (**to $700**), Stifel (**to $660**), and Wedbush (**$550 → $700**).[52806715][52806214] **Palo Alto Networks (PANW)** also traded lower **(-3.97%)**, reinforcing the ZS-driven derisking theme even as Benchmark and Wedbush lifted price targets and reiterated Buy/Outperform ratings.[52809853][52806158][52821535]

Data, observability, and developer tooling were choppy into Snowflake’s earnings: **SNOW -1.71%**, **MDB -3.66%**, **DDOG -1.00%**, and **ESTC -0.47%** traded unevenly as investors positioned ahead of SNOW’s after‑hours print.[52827161] For **Snowflake**, the Street went in looking for **$0.32 EPS vs $0.24** YoY and **$1.32B revenue vs $1.04B** YoY, with particular focus on AI workloads, Cortex, and data‑sharing, and with the company having beaten revenue in 20+ consecutive quarters.[52812375][52820272] The stock still closed lower on the day at **$175.17**, but the subsequent Q1 release showed an AI‑fueled beat that dramatically changed the setup: Q1 revenue landed at **$1.391B** vs **$1.324B** est, with adjusted EPS **$0.39 vs $0.32** est; product revenue was **$1.33B (+34% YoY)**, RPO **$9.21B (+38%)**, net revenue retention **126%**, and 779 customers with >**$1M** TTM product revenue, after 616 net new customers in the quarter.[52825919][52824228] Management guided Q2 product revenue to **$1.415–$1.42B** (~**30%** growth), with **12.5%** adjusted operating margin in Q2 and **13.5%** for FY27, and raised FY27 product revenue guidance to **$5.84B** (+**31%** YoY) alongside a higher full‑year operating margin target of **13.5%**; non‑GAAP operating margin was already **12%** in Q1, up over **300 bps** YoY.[52825919][52831090] On the strategic side, Snowflake announced a new **multi‑year strategic collaboration** with **AWS**, including a **$6B multi‑year infrastructure commitment** aimed at accelerating enterprise agentic AI adoption and optimizing cloud compute, brought CoCo (Cortex Code) to GA (now at **7,100+ active accounts**), and signed a deal to acquire **Natoma**, a Model Context Protocol platform to connect AI agents with enterprise apps, databases, APIs, and tools.[52831090][52825919][52824465] The company also disclosed that co‑founder **Benoit Dageville** will step away from day‑to‑day operations in mid‑June while remaining on the board, and BTIG’s Gray Powell reiterated **Buy** while raising his PT from **$235 to $280**.[52827490][52831376] Despite SNOW’s weak regular‑session tape, shares ripped **~33–37% after‑hours** (to roughly **$233–$241**) and were up **~35%+** in Thursday premarket, underscoring a major positive repricing relative to the 5/27 close.[52831354][52826894][52831090]

Within that same data/observability complex, **MongoDB (MDB)** fell **-3.66%** even as Canaccord Genuity raised its price target from **$375 to $400** and kept a Buy rating, suggesting that incremental positive sell‑side wasn’t enough to counter broader software de‑risking into the Snowflake event.[52812686][52831354] **Datadog (DDOG)** slipped **-1.00%** despite announcing that “Datadog for Government” secured **FedRAMP High** authorization via Carahsoft, opening up additional public‑sector TAM, again highlighting risk‑off behavior toward high‑multiple observability names.[52805732][52831354] **Elastic (ESTC)** was down a modest **-0.47%** as JPM cut its PT from **$99 to $80** while maintaining an Overweight rating, reflecting a reset in AI search/logging expectations but not a thesis change.[52808813][52831354]

CRM and broader application software added to the cautious tone. **Salesforce (CRM)** dipped **-0.70%** to **$178.94** into an after‑hours Q1 print that delivered a sizable EPS beat but a more measured top‑line outlook. Ahead of the release, expectations were for **$3.12 EPS** on **$11.05–$11.06B** revenue and for Salesforce to showcase AI agents and automation as key budget drivers; options flow showed notable bearish positioning, including a CRM call sweep in the **$180** strike expiring **05/29/26**.[52820272][52818901] Reported Q1 FY27 results came in at adjusted EPS **$3.88 vs $3.12** est (+24% beat), up **50%+ YoY**, on revenue of **$11.133B vs $11.052B** est (+13% YoY), with total RPO at **$67.9B** (+11% YoY), operating cash flow **$6.7B**, free cash flow **$6.6B**, and cash/cash equivalents **$8.94B**.[52823920][52825136] Management guided Q2 revenue to **$11.27–$11.35B** vs **$11.36B** est and adjusted EPS to **$3.25–$3.27** vs **$3.24** est, and set FY27 revenue guidance at **$45.9–$46.2B** vs **$46.121B** est while raising full‑year EPS guidance to **$14.06–$14.12** from **$13.11–$13.19**, well above the **$13.22** Street consensus.[52824209][52824229] On the call, CEO **Marc Benioff** described “record revenue, record deals and cash flow,” framed agentic AI as Salesforce’s biggest growth opportunity, and dismissed “SaaSpocalypse” fears, while CFO commentary flagged softness in **Commerce Cloud** and **Tableau** as key offsets to AI‑driven strength.[52825136][52830159] Premium AI SKUs tied to **Agentforce** saw bookings grow nearly **60% YoY**, with Agentforce ARR crossing roughly **$1B** and ARR growth cited at about **205%** to **$1.2B**, and Salesforce executed a **$25B** accelerated share repurchase that cut diluted share count by **10% YoY**, with upfront delivery of **103M** shares (~80% of the ASR) and final settlement expected in **Q3 FY27**.[52830159][52824606] Despite the strong print and capital‑return story, CRM traded **-3.22% after‑hours to $171.80** and was noted **-1.86% premarket** on Thursday, as investors focused on the cautious revenue outlook and legacy cloud drag; Piper Sandler’s Billy Fitzsimmons reiterated **Overweight** with a **$215** PT into the weakness.[52825136][52831144][52831354][52829008]
