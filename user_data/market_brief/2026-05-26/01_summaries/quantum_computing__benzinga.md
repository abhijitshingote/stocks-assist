# Quantum Computing — benzinga

_kind: theme · elapsed: 195.8s_

Quantum computing stocks ripped higher Friday as investors digested a major U.S. **$2 billion CHIPS Act quantum package** and an emerging **quantum foundry and hardware build‑out**, even as **Nvidia (NVDA) slipped -2.27%** on profit‑taking after a blowout AI quarter. The move tied together three pillars of the theme: **quantum hardware platforms**, **error‑correction and AI‑assisted quantum stacks**, and **federal funding for dedicated quantum manufacturing capacity**.

Quantum hardware pure‑plays and software‑adjacent names **RGTI +20.59%, QBTS +12.13, IONQ +7.53%, QUBT +6.98%, ARQQ +6.06%** dramatically outperformed a firm tech tape (**SPY +0.39%, QQQ +0.42%, SOXX +2.33%, IGV +1.65%, XLK +1.00%**) alongside quantum‑linked foundries and incumbents **GFS +6.28%, IBM +1.72%, INTC +1.26%**, while AI bellwether **NVDA -2.27%** consolidated after “blockbuster” results.[partial1][partial2][partial3][partial4]

### U.S. CHIPS quantum package and foundry build‑out (IBM, Anderon, GFS, RGTI, QBTS)

The central catalyst was the **U.S. Department of Commerce’s $2 billion CHIPS Act commitment to nine quantum companies**, with **IBM as the largest recipient at about $1 billion** and smaller allocations to **GlobalFoundries (GFS), Rigetti (RGTI), D‑Wave (QBTS), Atom Computing, Diraq, Infleqtion, PsiQuantum and Quantinuum**.[partial2][partial4] IBM will use its award to build **Anderon**, billed as **America’s first pure‑play quantum chip wafer foundry** producing **300‑mm quantum wafers in Albany, NY**, and will **match the federal money with $1 billion of its own capital plus IP and staff**, while the government takes a minority, non‑controlling equity stake.[partial3][partial4] The Anderon announcement drove IBM’s **best week since October 2002**, including a roughly **12% one‑day gain** on Thursday and a week‑to‑date move of about **15%**, with historical studies showing IBM has usually been higher one month and one year after prior ≥15% weekly spikes.[partial3][partial4] Media commentary and CNBC “Final Trades” callouts framed IBM’s CHIPS‑backed foundry role as a structural quantum and AI infrastructure play rather than a transient trading pop.[partial4]

The same Commerce package **explicitly includes Rigetti and D‑Wave** and **names GlobalFoundries as a smaller quantum beneficiary**, reinforcing that U.S. industrial policy is not only funding qubit R&D but also seeding a **domestic quantum manufacturing stack**.[partial2][partial3][partial4] **GlobalFoundries** complemented its federal support by launching **Quantum Technology Solutions**, a new unit “aimed at scaling U.S. quantum computing manufacturing capabilities,” alongside a **$375M CHIPS Act LOI** and a **~1% strategic equity stake** from Commerce, positioning GFS as a key U.S. quantum manufacturing hub.[partial2] GFS is also partnering with **Broadcom, Applied Materials, Meta and Synopsys** in a **$125 million UCLA AI Semiconductor Hub** focused on AI‑powered chip technologies across design, manufacturing, packaging, advanced materials and cloud infrastructure, a linkage that ties quantum process development directly into broader AI‑chip innovation and workforce pipelines.[partial4]

### Quantum hardware cohort: valuation, short interest and CHIPS‑driven squeeze (IONQ, RGTI, QBTS, QUBT, ARQQ)

Within the pure‑play quantum complex, the Commerce package triggered and extended a **high‑beta squeeze across heavily shorted names**:

- **Rigetti (RGTI +20.59%, close $25.80)**: RGTI extended a thin‑float surge after Thursday’s ~30% jump tied to the CHIPS quantum allocation and equity grants, plus a **technical breakout above the key $21.35 level** that had acted as both support and resistance over the past 18 months.[partial3] Benzinga’s technical note argued Friday’s action suggested this resistance “may be breaking,” implying prior sellers have been cleared out and buyers must push prices higher to find supply.[partial3] Fundamentally, RGTI is also cited as a valuation outlier with a **~$8.8B market cap vs. just $23M expected revenue this year and $44M next year**, highlighting the risk of a funding‑driven bubble even as federal support de‑risks the company’s runway.[partial2]

- **D‑Wave Quantum (QBTS +12.13%, close $28.38)**: QBTS followed through after **+33% on Thursday** on news that D‑Wave will receive Commerce Department **equity funding** as part of the roughly **$2B quantum package**.[partial3] In parallel, D‑Wave signed a **Letter of Intent with Commerce for $100M of proposed funding** to accelerate both its **annealing and gate‑model systems**, giving it both CHIPS inclusion and a dedicated programmatic LOI.[partial2] That two‑layer federal backing drove QBTS into the list of top large‑cap gainers last week.[partial2]

- **IonQ (IONQ +7.53%, close $62.54)**: IonQ rallied as the **largest pure‑play quantum name**, described with a **$23B valuation**, **24% short interest**, and revenue projected at **$268M** this year, with a **forward P/E of 193**.[partial2] Articles explicitly compare current quantum valuations, including IonQ’s, to **prior cannabis bubbles**, warning that the underlying **$1.4–$5.8B quantum market today** (QED‑C) and **$35B 10‑year projection** may not justify present multiples absent significant upside surprises.[partial2] Beyond CHIPS read‑through, IONQ also picked up incremental interest as a top holding in the **Tuttle Capital UFO Disclosure ETF (UFOD)**, which tags IonQ as a “quantum play” leveraged to U.S. military modernization alongside defense names like Palantir, Rocket Lab and Lockheed Martin.[partial4]

- **Quantum Computing Inc. (QUBT +6.98%, close $12.18)**: QUBT continued higher with the group; it has **~30% short interest** and is cited among quantum names that have “more than doubled” since March lows, with the latest leg explicitly tied to the Commerce **$2B** package.[partial2] Earlier in the week, QUBT was highlighted as rallying sharply as part of the IBM‑ and Rigetti‑led quantum push that also lifted the **QTUM** ETF.[partial3]

- **Arqit Quantum (ARQQ +6.06%, close $17.59)**: ARQQ moved as a **thin‑float sympathy play**, with flows supported by the CHIPS quantum announcement and ETF inflows into quantum and semiconductor products like **QTUM** and **SOXX**, even though Arqit itself was not named among the federal award recipients.[partial2][partial3][partial4]

Across the group, **short interest is elevated** — **IONQ 24%, RGTI 16%, QBTS 16%, QUBT 30%** — and prices have already **re‑rated sharply since March**: IONQ is cited as **+>145%**, RGTI **+110%**, and QBTS and QUBT as having “more than doubled.”[partial2] This setup provides “ample fuel” for further **squeeze‑style continuation** but also “air pockets post‑catalyst,” with commentators repeatedly flagging the risk that quantum equities could retrace like prior speculative manias if revenue ramps disappoint.[partial2]

### Quantum ETFs and ecosystem read‑through (QTUM, SOXX, SMH)

ETF flows both reflected and amplified the move. The **Defiance Quantum ETF (QTUM)** has grown to **> $4 billion AUM** and carries a **5‑star Morningstar rating**, billed as a liquid way to access a basket of **quantum, AI, semis and enabling infrastructure**.[partial3] On Friday it was **up 2.6%**, with single‑name contributors including IBM, RGTI, QBTS, IONQ and QUBT quoted up strongly intraday.[partial3] Benzinga notes that broader semiconductor ETFs such as **SOXX** and **SMH** “could benefit” from the expanding **quantum‑related fabrication and manufacturing build‑out**, as CHIPS funding and university‑industry consortia like UCLA’s AI hub feed into advanced node and quantum process flows.[partial3]

### Nvidia and AI infrastructure: from sovereign AI to quantum error correction

While the quantum pure‑plays squeezed, **Nvidia (NVDA)** spent Friday consolidating **-2.27% (close $215.23)**, even as its latest quarter underlined its central role in **AI infrastructure and, increasingly, quantum‑adjacent tooling**. For fiscal Q1, NVDA reported **$81.6–81.615B in revenue, up ~85% YoY** and ahead of ~$79B Street estimates, with **adjusted EPS $1.87 vs. ~$1.76–1.77 expected** and **data‑center revenue ~ $75.2B (~92% of total)** at **~75% non‑GAAP gross margin**.[partial2][partial3] Management guided **July‑quarter revenue to about $91B**, nearly 2x the prior year and described as “staggering,” with some notes suggesting guidance may be conservative because it excludes portions of China business.[partial2][partial3] Alongside the beat, NVDA **raised its quarterly dividend from $0.01 to $0.25 per share (25x increase, $1.00 annual)** and authorized an additional **$80B share‑repurchase program**, drawing comparisons to Apple’s capital‑return profile.[partial1][partial2][partial3]

Despite the fundamentals, NVDA’s stock has been **sold on strength**. Articles describe post‑earnings trading where shares fell **2.84%** immediately after the print, and on Friday the name was framed as **pulling back toward $215 support**, just above a cited **$212 technical level**, following a peak near **$236**.[partial1][partial2] Chart commentary casts the move as a **“break‑and‑retest”** of prior resistance turned support, with a medium‑term upside scenario back toward all‑time highs and potentially **$300** in line with Street price targets from Wedbush, Benchmark and Truist.[partial1][partial2] At the same time, prediction‑market coverage notes that even at a **$5.4T market cap** and with NVDA having **beaten estimates 14 straight quarters**, investor sentiment shows “marked apathy” and a rotation toward secondary and tertiary AI names, which dovetails with the **relative outperformance of INTC, GFS and the quantum cohort**.[partial1][partial3]

From a **quantum‑specific** perspective, NVDA is also pushing into **AI‑assisted quantum error correction**. Benzinga highlights that Nvidia recently launched **“Ising,” a family of AI models that decodes quantum errors up to 2.5 times faster**, a tool aimed at accelerating quantum error‑correction workflows on the path to practical, fault‑tolerant machines.[partial3] This positions NVDA not as a direct qubit manufacturer but as a provider of **accelerated classical and AI infrastructure that supports quantum stacks**, aligning with research themes where **AI helps decode error syndromes and optimize surface‑code or lattice‑surgery schemes**.

At the infrastructure level, a **CNAS report** cited by Benzinga finds Nvidia supplies GPUs for **52% of tracked sovereign AI projects worldwide**, including systems like the **Abu Dhabi sovereign AI cloud**, Poland’s **AGH Cyfronet Helios** supercomputer and Japan’s **ABCI 3.0** upgrade.[partial3] The report stresses that U.S. firms **NVDA, AMD, INTC, DELL, HPE, ORCL, AMZN and CSCO** dominate accelerators, servers, cloud and networking layers of sovereign AI stacks, implying that the same vendors will likely underpin **government‑backed quantum and post‑quantum infrastructure** as those systems mature.[partial3] Separate macro commentary positions the broader **AI build‑out as an ~$8T capex cycle with only 12–18% completed**, with bottlenecks spreading from GPUs into memory, cooling, copper, fiber and power, and explicitly identifies **platform and connectivity names and NVDA** as the “defensive” corner of AI versus more speculative memory and high‑beta trades.[partial1]

NVDA’s otherwise dominant narrative is tempered by **China‑specific headwinds**. CEO Jensen Huang recently said Nvidia’s AI chip market share in China has plunged from **about 90% to zero**, as export controls and local procurement policies hampered shipments of China‑specific parts like the **H20** and even gaming‑class **RTX 5090D V2**.[partial4] Commentary suggests Nvidia has “largely conceded” the China AI market and has warned that strict U.S. sanctions risk accelerating domestic Chinese alternatives, potentially bifurcating global compute stacks into Western and Chinese ecosystems.[partial1][partial4]
